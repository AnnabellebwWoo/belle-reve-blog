I made the interesting choice of visiting SF in the middle of finals, but I figured I might as well make the most of it. The purpose of the trip was for a food sustainability conference (which you can read more about [here](https://www.belle-reve.com/blog/post/stanford-f4t)), but when I wasn't attending events I was studying or exploring the area. 

This was my first completely solo trip, and reflecting on it, my favourite part was orginizing the logistics. Little things like the esim, public transit, and a low fees debit card was deliciously satisfying. I mean, they're just day-to-day things but it's like the satisfaction of cleaning your room or completing a project. 

It started with a red-eye from Toronto to San Francisco. The flight was pretty empty and I even got a row to myself. Once I touched down, I commuted to campus and even took this fancy shuttle bus decked out in Stanford branding. My first introduction to the bay area was the shear amount of people coding on the Caltrain. 

![on the way](/images/otw.jpg)

![stanford shuttle](/images/fancy-bus.jpg)

Once conference festivities wrapped up, I needed a dinner spot. Knowing that this was likely the only meal I was purchasing this weekend, I knew I had to go with some Mexican food. I chose a local place called [Taqueria El Grullense M&G](https://maps.app.goo.gl/YDZ7HHgk9fgHnjny5). It felt jarring to be eating a veggie burrito in Palo Alto when just a couple hours ago I was sitting in an still exam hall checking over my work. Also my handwash-only wool sweater smelled like this burrito for the rest of the trip. 

![palo alto sunset](/images/sunset.jpg)

Although most attendees commutted via Uber, I opted for public transit to see more of the area and keep costs low. It was easy to navigate and the peaceful people watching broke up the vigorously-social events nicely. 

On Saturday, I grabbed some coffee from the hotel in San Jose and headed back to Stanford. I spent the morning strolling around campus and studying outside. And I have to note, the campus is huge! Most students biked just to get around. This is a stark contrast to McMaster, where it takes 15 minutes, at most, to get across the furthest points on campus. 

![stanford statues](/images/statues.jpg)

![stanford church](/images/church.jpg)

I also had to check out the campus store, which has three levels to fill all your merch desires. 

![stanford book store](/images/merch.jpg)

I connected with some SF-locals at dinner to make the ultimate tourist plan for the next day. After goodbyes, I walked around campus some more to take in the quiet stillness at night. With a lot of conference attendees leaving that night, I got the hotel room to myself! 

Sunday had no committments so I was free to explore by myself. I grabbed an apple and some coffee at the hotel and started with checking my bag at a convenience store. Then headed north for downtown SF. My first stop was Ghiradelli Square. I picked up some postcards and did some window shopping. Then I walked west along the pier towards the Golden Gate Bridge.

Some miscellaneous notes:

- a surplus of bike shops
- waymos took me by surprise
- AI conversations everywhere; lunches on campus, bus rides, and everywhere in-between
- higher concentration of tech ads on buses, transit stops, billboards instead of something like food or personal consumer products
